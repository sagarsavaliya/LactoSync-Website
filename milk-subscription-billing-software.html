Why Milk Billing Is Hard

Milk Is the Hardest Subscription to Bill

It is a daily product with a monthly invoice, and almost every customer's month is slightly different from every other customer's month.

The quantity changes constantly

One litre most days, two when guests come, none for the ten days the family was away. A monthly bill is the sum of thirty different days, not a fixed amount.

Every customer has their own rate

Cow and buffalo differ, long-standing customers have old rates, and bulk households were promised something else. Held in one person's head, this does not survive a busy month.

Rate revisions land mid-month

When the rate changes on the 12th, every bill needs splitting across two rates. Done by hand, this is where most billing errors come from.

Advances and dues drift apart

Some customers pay in advance, some settle partly, some carry an old balance. Without a ledger per customer, nobody is certain who owes what.

Billing eats the first week

Copying a month of deliveries into bills takes evenings. During those evenings, collection has not started — so cash arrives later every month.

Chasing payment is a personal cost

Reminder calls to neighbours and long-standing customers are uncomfortable, so they get delayed — and receivables quietly grow.

How a Bill Is Built

From 30 Deliveries to One Invoice

Nothing in this sequence requires you to remember anything.

1. The subscription sets the expectation

A customer is set up once: two litres of cow milk, every morning. That standing order drives the delivery list from the next day onwards.

2. The day's reality is recorded

Riders mark what was actually delivered — including the day a customer asked for an extra litre, and the week they were away. The bill will follow the record, not the assumption.

3. Rates are applied per customer

Each delivered quantity is priced from that customer's own rate card. If the rate changed on the 12th, days before and after are priced separately without anyone splitting the month by hand.

4. The invoice generates

At month end, every customer's invoice is produced together — itemised by day, totalled, with GST applied where it is due. What took several evenings becomes a single action.

5. It reaches the customer, and the reminder follows

Bills go out on WhatsApp on the same day. If a bill stays unpaid, reminders go on their own, so collection starts early in the month and does not depend on anyone making an uncomfortable call.

6. Payment closes the loop

Cash taken at the door, a UPI transfer or a bank payment is recorded against the customer's ledger. Advances adjust automatically against the next bill, and the outstanding figure is always current.

The delivery records that feed these invoices come from the milk delivery management side of LactoSync, and owners review it all from the dairy owner app. Customers see their own bills in the customer app.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is milk subscription and billing software?
Milk subscription and billing software keeps each customer's standing daily order — product, quantity, shift and schedule — and turns the deliveries actually made against that order into a monthly invoice. It handles pauses, one-off quantity changes, per-customer rates and mid-month rate revisions automatically, then sends the bill to the customer and tracks the payment against their ledger.
How does LactoSync calculate a monthly milk bill?
The bill is built from the delivery record, not from the subscription alone. Every delivery marked by a rider is priced using that customer's own rate card for that date, days on pause are excluded, and one-off extras are included. The result is an itemised, day-wise invoice, with GST applied where it is due.
What happens if the milk rate changes in the middle of the month?
You enter the new rate with the date it takes effect. Deliveries before that date are priced at the old rate and deliveries after it at the new one, on every affected customer's bill, without anyone splitting the month by hand. This is the single most common source of manual billing errors, and it disappears.
Can customers receive their milk bills on WhatsApp?
Yes. Bills are sent to customers over WhatsApp on paid plans, with the day-by-day breakdown included, and automated payment reminders follow if a bill stays unpaid. Customers can also open their current bill, delivery calendar and payment history any time in the LactoSync customer app.
Does LactoSync track advance payments and outstanding dues?
Yes. Each customer has a running ledger covering cash, UPI and bank payments. Advances are adjusted automatically against the next bill, partial payments leave the correct balance outstanding, and you can see total receivables and how long each amount has been pending at any time.
Can we bill different customers at different rates?
Yes. Rates are stored per customer and per product, so old customers on legacy rates, bulk households and cow-versus-buffalo pricing are all handled correctly without anyone having to remember who pays what.
Does LactoSync generate GST invoices?
Yes, GST is applied on invoices where it is due, and LactoSync's own subscription is billed to you with a proper GST tax invoice each month. Plan prices shown on the site exclude GST; 18% GST is added at billing.
Can we import our existing customers and their subscriptions?
Yes. During the guided setup we import your existing customer list with their quantities and rates rather than making you type them in, so you are not starting from an empty screen.
