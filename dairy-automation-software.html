First, a Definition

What "Dairy Automation" Actually Means Here

Not robots in the shed. The paperwork and coordination that surround the milk.

For most Indian dairies, the hours do not go into milking. They go into everything that happens around it: working out how much milk each route needs, telling riders who paused, writing down what was delivered, copying a month of deliveries into bills, chasing payments, and answering the same customer questions on the phone.

Dairy automation software removes the re-entry. A fact is recorded once, at the moment it happens, and every downstream document builds itself from that record. A delivery marked at a doorstep at 6am is the same record that produces the month's invoice, updates the customer's ledger, adjusts tomorrow's loading quantity and appears in your revenue report — without anyone copying it anywhere.

That is the whole idea. Everything below is a consequence of it.

The Practical Difference

The Same Dairy, Two Different Days

A 250-customer dairy running two shifts.

Before — the owner is the system

The morning starts with phone calls: how much to load, who paused, which house complained yesterday. Deliveries are written in a register that evening, if there is time. Pauses are remembered or not. At month end, three evenings go into copying the register into bills, and a fourth into arguing about four of them. Collection starts in the second week, so cash always arrives late. Nobody can say what the total outstanding is without adding it up.

After — the record is the system

Loading quantities are on the rider's phone before 4:30am, pauses already removed. The round is marked stop by stop, offline where there is no signal. Cash taken at the door is recorded against the customer immediately. On the 1st, every invoice generates in one action and goes out on WhatsApp the same day, so collection starts on day one instead of day ten. Disputes are settled by sharing a dated delivery list. The owner opens a dashboard rather than a notebook.

Each of these areas has its own detail page: milk delivery management and subscription & billing. The three apps that make it work are the owner app, rider app and customer app.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is dairy automation software?
Dairy automation software records each fact once and builds every downstream document from it, instead of having staff re-enter the same information in a register, a bill book and a payment diary. In LactoSync, a delivery marked at a doorstep produces the customer's monthly invoice, updates their payment ledger, adjusts the next day's loading quantity and feeds the revenue report — with no copying in between.
Does dairy automation mean I need machinery or special hardware?
No. LactoSync automates the administrative side of a dairy — daily milk requirement, delivery rounds, subscriptions, billing, payments, customer communication and reporting. It runs on the Android phones your staff already carry, plus a web dashboard for owners. There is nothing to install in the shed and no equipment to buy.
How much time does automating a dairy actually save?
The two large blocks are the daily coordination that runs through the owner's phone every morning, and the several evenings that go into month-end billing. Dairies typically get both back: billing becomes a single action instead of a multi-evening job, and route decisions stop needing phone calls because the manifest is already on each rider's phone.
Will my delivery staff be able to use it?
The rider app deliberately shows one thing at a time — today's route, in delivery order, with a tap to mark each stop. It requires no technical knowledge and works fully offline. Most riders are comfortable after a single round.
Does it work if our area has poor mobile network?
Yes. The delivery app is built to work offline, which is the part of the day where connectivity is least reliable. Riders mark deliveries with no signal at all and the data syncs automatically once the phone reconnects. The owner's web dashboard does need a connection.
Can LactoSync track milk production as well as sales?
Yes. Milk production and inventory are tracked alongside customer consumption, so you can compare what was produced or procured against what was actually delivered and sold, and see the gap rather than estimate it.
Is LactoSync suitable for a small dairy, or only large ones?
It is used by dairies from around twenty customers up to cooperatives serving over five hundred. Pricing is a flat monthly plan based on customer count, starting at ₹499 per month plus GST for up to 200 customers, so a small dairy is not paying for scale it does not have.
What does it cost to automate our dairy with LactoSync?
Flat monthly plans, priced by customer count: Basic ₹499 (up to 200 customers), Starter ₹699 (up to 300), Growth ₹899 (up to 400) and Pro ₹1,099 (up to 500), each plus 18% GST. Custom Enterprise pricing applies beyond 500 customers. There are no per-delivery fees, no per-message charges and no commission on your collection.
